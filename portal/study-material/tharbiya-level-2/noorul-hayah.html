Becoming a Good Person

Our beloved Prophet ﷺ had the most beautiful character. In this book, each lesson teaches one beautiful quality — a story, why it matters, an āyah from the Qur'an, a hadith, a story for the heart, and one thing to try today.

Let's begin →
LESSON 1 OF 10
🗣️

Speaking the Truth

A truthful person is loved by Allah and trusted by everyone around them. When we always tell the truth — even when it is difficult, even when we made a mistake — our heart stays clean and calm. Lying might feel easy for a moment, but it grows into bigger problems and takes away people's trust. A true Muslim is known for honesty in words and actions. Choose the truth, and you will always walk with a light heart.

💡 Why it matters

When people know you always tell the truth, they trust you completely — your word becomes as strong as a promise. Trust is like a beautiful glass: honesty keeps it shining, but one lie can crack it, and cracks are hard to mend.

  • ✦Truth brings peace to your heart
  • ✦A small lie grows into a big problem
  • ✦Allah loves and rewards the truthful
✦ From the Qur'an

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا اتَّقُوا اللَّهَ وَكُونُوا مَعَ الصَّادِقِينَ

O you who believe! Fear Allah and be with those who are truthful.

At-Tawbah 9:119

✿ From the Sunnah

"Truthfulness leads to righteousness, and righteousness leads to Paradise."

— Bukhari & Muslim

❤ A story for the heart

The Broken Window

Yusuf was playing cricket in the yard when his ball flew straight through the neighbour's window. Glass shattered everywhere. His heart pounded — no one had seen him. He could run away and stay silent. But he remembered that Allah always sees. Slowly, he walked to the neighbour's door and knocked. 'Uncle, I broke your window. I'm very sorry. I'll help pay for it.' The old man looked at him for a long moment, then smiled warmly. 'Son, the window costs a little money. But your honesty is worth more than a hundred windows. Not many boys would knock on this door.' Yusuf walked home lighter than air — his pocket empty of excuses, his heart full of peace.

🌱 Try this today

For one whole day, keep a 'truth promise'. Every time you feel tempted to hide something or exaggerate, stop and say the honest thing instead. At night, tell a parent one moment you chose the truth.

🤝

Trustworthiness

Amāna means being trustworthy — when someone gives you something to keep, or asks you to do a task, you protect it and complete it faithfully. A trustworthy child returns what they borrow, keeps promises, and does not peek at secrets. People feel safe around someone they can trust. The Prophet ﷺ was called 'Al-Amīn' — the trustworthy one — even before he became a Prophet. When you are trustworthy, you carry a piece of his beautiful character.

💡 Why it matters

A trustworthy person becomes the safe place everyone runs to. People leave their most precious things with them, because they know nothing will be lost or harmed. This is one of the highest honours a person can earn — and it is built one kept promise at a time.

  • ✦Keep every promise, big or small
  • ✦Return what you borrow, exactly as you got it
  • ✦Guard secrets and duties faithfully
✦ From the Qur'an

إِنَّ اللَّهَ يَأْمُرُكُمْ أَن تُؤَدُّوا الْأَمَانَاتِ إِلَىٰ أَهْلِهَا

Indeed, Allah commands you to render trusts to whom they are due.

An-Nisa 4:58

✿ From the Sunnah

"The signs of a hypocrite are three: when he speaks he lies, when he promises he breaks it, and when he is trusted he betrays."

— Bukhari & Muslim

❤ A story for the heart

The One Everyone Trusted

Long before he became a Prophet, the people of Makkah called Muhammad ﷺ 'Al-Amīn' — the Trustworthy One. Even those who later opposed him would still leave their gold, silver and valuables with him for safekeeping, because they knew he would never touch what was not his. On the very night he had to leave Makkah, with enemies waiting outside his door, he still made sure every trust in his care was returned to its owner. He asked Ali to stay behind just to hand back people's belongings. Imagine — even his enemies trusted him with their treasures! That is the beauty of amāna: it makes even those who dislike you rely on you.

🌱 Try this today

Ask a parent to lend you something valuable to them for the day — a book, a key, a small tool. Guard it carefully and return it in the evening exactly as you received it.

LESSON 3 OF 10
👨‍👩‍👧

Honouring Parents

After Allah, no one deserves our love and respect more than our parents. They cared for us when we were small and helpless, stayed awake for us, and gave without counting. Honouring them means speaking softly, obeying them in good things, helping without being asked, and never rolling our eyes or raising our voice. Even a kind look at your parents is rewarded. Their happiness with you brings Allah's happiness too.

💡 Why it matters

Your parents gave you more than you can ever count — nights without sleep, food from their own plate, worry when you were sick. Honouring them is a way of saying 'thank you' that never ends, and Allah tied His own pleasure to their pleasure with you.

  • ✦Speak softly, never raise your voice at them
  • ✦Help before they ask
  • ✦Make du'ā for them every day
✦ From the Qur'an

وَقَضَىٰ رَبُّكَ أَلَّا تَعْبُدُوا إِلَّا إِيَّاهُ وَبِالْوَالِدَيْنِ إِحْسَانًا

Your Lord has decreed that you worship none but Him, and be good to parents.

Al-Isra 17:23

✿ From the Sunnah

"The pleasure of the Lord lies in the pleasure of the parent, and the anger of the Lord lies in the anger of the parent."

— Tirmidhi

❤ A story for the heart

The Glass of Milk

It was past midnight, and little Aisha woke up thirsty. Half-asleep, she shuffled to the kitchen — and stopped. Her mother was sitting alone in the dark, quietly making du'ā, tears on her cheeks. On the table was a single glass of milk. 'Ummi, why are you awake?' Aisha whispered. Her mother wiped her eyes and smiled. 'I saved this milk for you, my love. There was only enough for one, so I kept it for you.' Aisha's throat tightened. All this time, her mother had gone without, just so her daughter would have. That night, Aisha carried the glass to her mother's lips and said, 'No, Ummi — today you drink, and I will make du'ā for you.' They shared it, laughing softly in the dark, hearts full.

🌱 Try this today

Do one helpful task for each parent today without being asked — and do it with a smile. Then say 'thank you for everything' to each of them before bed.

Respecting Teachers & Elders

Our teachers open the doors of knowledge for us, and our elders carry years of wisdom. Islam teaches us to respect them: to greet them first, listen quietly when they speak, stand to welcome them, and never argue rudely. We give up our seat for an elder, help them carry things, and speak to them gently. Respecting those older and wiser than us is a sign of a noble heart — and one day, younger ones will respect you the same way.

💡 Why it matters

Teachers and elders have walked roads you have not yet reached. Respecting them opens the door to their knowledge and du'ā. And the respect you give today plants a seed — one day younger ones will treat you exactly the way you treated your elders.

  • ✦Greet elders first and stand to welcome them
  • ✦Listen quietly; never interrupt rudely
  • ✦Give your seat and help carry their load
✦ From the Qur'an

وَقُولُوا لِلنَّاسِ حُسْنًا

And speak to people good words.

Al-Baqarah 2:83

✿ From the Sunnah

"He is not one of us who does not show mercy to our young and does not respect our elders."

— Tirmidhi

❤ A story for the heart

The Boy on the Prophet's Right

One day the Prophet ﷺ was given a drink. He took a sip, and on his right sat a young boy, while older, respected elders sat on his left. By custom, the drink should pass to the right — but the boy was young. Politely, the Prophet ﷺ turned to the boy and asked, 'Do you permit me to give it to the elders first?' He did not just take the boy's turn — he asked the child's permission, honouring his right. The little boy, though he could have let the elders drink, replied, 'No, O Messenger of Allah, I will not give up my share from you.' The Prophet ﷺ smiled and handed him the cup. See how respect flows both ways — the greatest man honoured a child's right, and taught elders and children alike how beautiful good manners can be.

🌱 Try this today

Tomorrow, greet your teacher warmly and thank them for one thing they taught you. At home, offer your seat or help to an elder before they ask.

Mercy & Gentleness

Allah is Ar-Rahman — the Most Merciful — and He loves those who are merciful. A gentle child speaks softly, is kind to animals, comforts a crying friend, and never bullies the weak. Gentleness does not mean weakness; it takes a strong, beautiful heart to stay soft when others are harsh. When you show mercy to those on earth, the One above the heavens shows mercy to you. Let kindness be your habit, even in small things.

💡 Why it matters

Mercy is a sign that Allah's light lives in your heart. The gentlest people are often the strongest, because it takes real courage to stay soft in a harsh world. And here is the promise: show mercy to creatures on earth, and the Most Merciful will pour His mercy on you.

  • ✦Be kind to animals and the weak
  • ✦Comfort anyone who is sad or hurt
  • ✦Gentleness is strength, not weakness
✦ From the Qur'an

وَمَا أَرْسَلْنَاكَ إِلَّا رَحْمَةً لِّلْعَالَمِينَ

And We have not sent you except as a mercy to the worlds.

Al-Anbiya 21:107

✿ From the Sunnah

"Those who are merciful will be shown mercy by the Most Merciful. Be merciful to those on the earth, and the One above the heavens will be merciful to you."

— Tirmidhi

❤ A story for the heart

Two Hearts, Two Endings

The Prophet ﷺ told his companions of two people. One was a woman who kept a cat locked up. She gave it no food and no freedom to hunt, until the poor creature grew weak and died of hunger. For that cruelty, the woman was punished. Then he told them of another — a man walking on a hot, dusty road, so thirsty he climbed into a well to drink. As he came out, he saw a dog panting, licking the dirt from terrible thirst. The man thought, 'This poor dog is as thirsty as I was.' So he climbed back down, filled his shoe with water, held it in his mouth as he climbed, and gave the dog a drink. For that one small mercy, the Prophet ﷺ said, Allah forgave all his sins. One kind act to a thirsty animal opened the gates of Paradise. Never think any act of mercy is too small.

🌱 Try this today

Do one gentle act today for a creature that cannot thank you — give water to a bird, move a bug to safety, or feed a stray cat. Notice how your heart feels afterwards.

LESSON 6 OF 10
🫶

Cooperation & Helping Others

Muslims are like one body — when we help each other, everyone grows stronger. Cooperation means sharing the work, lifting what is heavy together, and never leaving someone to struggle alone. Help your family with chores, help a classmate who is stuck, help a neighbour carry their bags. Allah keeps helping the servant as long as the servant keeps helping others. Small hands working together can do great things.

💡 Why it matters

Big tasks become light when many hands lift together. When we cooperate, no one is left struggling alone, and the whole group grows stronger and closer. Allah Himself keeps helping the person who keeps helping others — so every time you help, help comes back to you.

  • ✦Share the work; don't leave others alone
  • ✦Help without waiting to be asked
  • ✦Together, small hands do great things
✦ From the Qur'an

وَتَعَاوَنُوا عَلَى الْبِرِّ وَالتَّقْوَىٰ

And cooperate in righteousness and piety.

Al-Ma'idah 5:2

✿ From the Sunnah

"Allah helps the servant as long as the servant helps his brother."

— Muslim

❤ A story for the heart

The Heavy Bench

The class was told to move the heavy wooden benches to the other room before the teacher returned. One boy, Bilal, gripped a bench and pulled with all his might — but it barely moved, and his face went red with strain. The other children watched and giggled. Bilal kept struggling alone, sweat on his forehead. Then a quiet girl named Zahra put down her bag and took the other end. 'Let's lift together,' she said simply. Then another child joined, and another. Suddenly the impossible bench floated across the room as if it weighed nothing, and everyone was laughing — not at Bilal now, but with him. When the teacher returned, every bench was in place. 'How did you finish so fast?' she asked. Zahra smiled: 'We stopped watching, and started helping.'

🌱 Try this today

Find one person today who is doing a task alone and join them without being asked — clearing the table, tidying the class, carrying something. Finish it together.

Cleanliness

Cleanliness is half of faith. A Muslim keeps their body clean, their clothes neat, and their home and surroundings tidy. We wash before prayer, brush our teeth, keep our nails trimmed, and never throw litter on the ground. A clean child is pleasant to be near and pleasing to Allah, who loves purity. Even removing something harmful from a path is a good deed. When you are clean outside, it helps your heart feel clean inside too.

💡 Why it matters

Cleanliness is called half of faith because a believer keeps both body and soul pure. When your body, clothes and surroundings are clean, your mind feels clear and your worship feels fresh. Even clearing something harmful from a path is written down as a good deed.

  • ✦Keep your body, clothes and room clean
  • ✦Never litter; put waste in its place
  • ✦Removing harm from a path is charity
✦ From the Qur'an

إِنَّ اللَّهَ يُحِبُّ التَّوَّابِينَ وَيُحِبُّ الْمُتَطَهِّرِينَ

Indeed, Allah loves those who repent and those who purify themselves.

Al-Baqarah 2:222

✿ From the Sunnah

"Cleanliness is half of faith."

— Muslim

❤ A story for the heart

The Branch on the Road

The Prophet ﷺ once told of a man who was simply walking along a road when he found a thorny branch lying across the path. He thought of all the people who would walk there — barefoot children, tired travellers, the elderly — and how the thorns could hurt them. So he bent down and moved the branch out of the way. It was such a small thing that no one even saw him do it. But Allah saw. The Prophet ﷺ said that for this one act — just clearing a branch so others would not be harmed — Allah was so pleased that He forgave that man and admitted him to Paradise. Cleanliness is not only about ourselves; it is caring for everyone who comes after us. Next time you see litter, think: this could be my branch on the road.

🌱 Try this today

Pick one small area — your desk, your shelf, or your corner of the room — and make it spotless today. Then remove one piece of litter you find outside and bin it.

Patience

Sabr — patience — means staying calm and strong when things are hard. When you are waiting, when you lose a game, when someone upsets you, or when a task is difficult, a patient child breathes, stays gentle, and does not shout or give up. Allah is with the patient ones. Patience is like a seed: it feels slow, but it grows into beautiful fruit. Every time you hold your temper or keep trying, you become stronger and closer to Allah.

💡 Why it matters

Patience is the quiet strength that carries us through hard moments without breaking. When we wait calmly, hold our temper, or keep trying after failing, we grow stronger and closer to Allah — for He is with the patient ones. Every hard moment held with sabr becomes a reward stored with Allah.

  • ✦Breathe and stay calm when things go wrong
  • ✦Keep trying after you fail
  • ✦Allah is with the patient
✦ From the Qur'an

إِنَّ اللَّهَ مَعَ الصَّابِرِينَ

Indeed, Allah is with the patient.

Al-Baqarah 2:153

✿ From the Sunnah

"Whoever tries to be patient, Allah will make him patient. No one is given a gift better and wider than patience."

— Bukhari & Muslim

❤ A story for the heart

The Seed That Waited

Little Hamza planted a seed in a pot and watered it. The next morning he ran to check — nothing. He was upset. He watered it again and waited. Day after day, still only brown soil. 'It's broken!' he said, ready to throw it away. His grandfather gently stopped him. 'Hamza, under the soil, where your eyes cannot see, that seed is working hard. It is growing roots first, quietly, before it ever shows a single leaf. If you dig it up now to check, you will kill it. Sabr — give it time and keep caring for it.' Hamza sighed, but he trusted his grandfather. He kept watering, kept waiting, even when it seemed pointless. Then one bright morning — a tiny green shoot! It had been growing all along, where he could not see. Hamza learned that patience is trusting that good things are growing, even when nothing shows yet — in seeds, and in ourselves.

🌱 Try this today

Next time you feel angry or frustrated today, pause and take three slow breaths before doing anything. Then respond calmly. Tell someone at night how it went.

LESSON 9 OF 10

Manners of Speech

Our words are powerful — they can heal or hurt. A well-mannered child speaks kindly, says 'please' and 'thank you', does not interrupt, and never uses bad language, mockery, or backbiting. Before speaking, a wise Muslim asks: is it true, is it kind, is it needed? If not, it is better to stay silent. Good words are a charity. Let your tongue spread peace, not pain, and people will love to listen to you.

💡 Why it matters

Words are like arrows — once they leave your mouth, you cannot pull them back. A kind word can heal a whole day; a harsh word can wound for years. Before speaking, a wise Muslim weighs three things: is it true, is it kind, is it needed? If not, silence is the more beautiful choice.

  • ✦Say 'please', 'thank you', 'sorry'
  • ✦No mocking, backbiting or bad words
  • ✦If it isn't good, stay silent
✦ From the Qur'an

وَقُل لِّعِبَادِي يَقُولُوا الَّتِي هِيَ أَحْسَنُ

And tell My servants to say that which is best.

Al-Isra 17:53

✿ From the Sunnah

"Whoever believes in Allah and the Last Day should say what is good or remain silent."

— Bukhari & Muslim

❤ A story for the heart

The Nails in the Fence

A boy who often spoke harshly was given a bag of nails by his father. 'Every time you say an unkind word, hammer one nail into this fence.' The first day, the boy hammered in fifteen nails! But hammering was hard work, and slowly he began holding his tongue to avoid it. Soon he told his father, 'Today I said no unkind words at all.' His father smiled. 'Now, for every kind day, pull one nail out.' In time, all the nails were gone. Proudly the boy showed the empty fence. His father ran his hand over the wood — full of holes. 'You did well, my son. But look: the nails are gone, yet the holes remain. When we hurt others with words, even after we say sorry, a little mark can stay in their heart. So choose your words gently from the start.' The boy never forgot the fence full of holes.

🌱 Try this today

Today, say 'please' and 'thank you' every single time, and give one honest compliment to three different people. Catch yourself before any unkind word and swallow it.

Conduct with Friends

Good friends help each other become better people. A good friend shares, keeps secrets, forgives small mistakes, and never mocks or excludes others. Choose friends who remind you of Allah and good deeds, and be that kind of friend yourself. Stand up for a friend being treated unfairly, but never join them in doing wrong. On the Day of Judgement, true friends who helped each other do good will be together in happiness. Be the friend you would love to have.

💡 Why it matters

We slowly become like the friends we spend time with — their habits, words and hearts rub off on us. A good friend pulls you closer to Allah and to good deeds; a bad friend pulls you away. So choose friends who make you better, and be that same friend to others.

  • ✦Share, forgive and keep secrets
  • ✦Include those who are left out
  • ✦Choose friends who bring you closer to good
✦ From the Qur'an

الْأَخِلَّاءُ يَوْمَئِذٍ بَعْضُهُمْ لِبَعْضٍ عَدُوٌّ إِلَّا الْمُتَّقِينَ

Close friends, that Day, will be enemies to each other, except for the righteous.

Az-Zukhruf 43:67

✿ From the Sunnah

"A person is upon the religion of his close friend, so let each of you look at whom he befriends."

— Abu Dawud & Tirmidhi

❤ A story for the heart

The Empty Seat

There was a new boy in class named Idris, who spoke a different language and sat alone every lunch. The other children found him strange and left the seats beside him empty. Idris ate quietly, his eyes on his food, trying not to look lonely. One day, Ahmad noticed him — really noticed him — and remembered how it felt the time he himself was new. While his friends called him to their table, Ahmad picked up his lunch, walked over, and sat right beside Idris. 'Assalamu alaikum. Can I sit with you?' Idris looked up, surprised, and his whole face lit with a shy smile. Slowly they began to talk, half in words, half in gestures, laughing at their mistakes. The next day, two more children joined. By the end of the week, Idris's table was the fullest in the room. Ahmad had done nothing grand — he had simply filled one empty seat with kindness, and it changed everything.

🌱 Try this today

Do one kind thing for a friend today with no reason — share something, help them, or simply tell them you're glad they're your friend. Include someone who is often left out.
