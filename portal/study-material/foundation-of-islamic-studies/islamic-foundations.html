Lesson One · What We Believe

The Six Articles of Faith

أَرْكَانُ الإِيمَان

Every strong building begins with a strong foundation you cannot even see — the part hidden under the ground. Our faith is like that hidden foundation. In Arabic we call it Iman, and it lives quietly inside your heart. Iman means the important truths a Muslim believes, even though we cannot see them with our eyes.

Our beloved Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught us that there are six of these beliefs. Think of them as six shining stars. When all six are in your heart, your faith is complete and bright. Let's meet each one.

1
☝️

Belief in Allah

We believe there is only one God — Allah. He was not born and He has no children. He made the whole universe, and He alone deserves our worship. Nothing is like Him.

2
✨

Belief in the Angels مَلائِكة

Allah created angels from light. We cannot see them, but they never disobey Allah. Jibreel brought Allah's words to the prophets, and two angels stay with each of us to write what we do.

3
📖

Belief in the Holy Books كُتُب

Allah sent guidance in holy books: the Tawrah, the Zabur, and the Injeel came to earlier prophets. The Qur'an is the final book, sent to Muhammad ﷺ, and Allah keeps it perfect and unchanged forever.

4
🕌

Belief in the Prophets رُسُل

Allah sent many kind prophets to guide people — like Adam, Nuh, Ibrahim, Musa and Isa (peace be upon them all). Muhammad ﷺ is the very last prophet; no prophet will come after him.

5
🌅

Belief in the Last Day الآخِرة

One day this world will end. Allah will bring everyone back to life and ask about how we lived. Those who believed and did good will enter Jannah (Paradise). Allah is perfectly fair and endlessly merciful.

6
🌟

Belief in Allah's Plan قَدَر

Allah knows everything that has happened and everything that will happen. Nothing takes place without His knowledge and wisdom. So we work hard, make du'a, and trust that Allah's plan is always best.

💡
Did you know?

Two angels travel with you all day long. One writes down your good deeds and the other your mistakes. So even a small kindness that nobody sees — like helping your mother or feeding a cat — is never, ever lost.

Build your Star of Faith ⭐

On a big sheet of paper, draw a six-pointed star. Give each point one Article of Faith, in your own words.

  1. Colour each point a different colour.
  2. Write the word IMAN in the middle.
  3. Show it to a parent or teacher and explain each point out loud.

The point you explain most easily is the one you understand best. Which was the hardest to explain?

🔑 New words to remember

Iman إيمان — faith / beliefMalaikah ملائكة — angelsQur'an قرآن — Allah's final bookAkhirah آخرة — the Last Day
⭐
Remember

Iman is what we believe in our heart. There are six Articles of Faith, and the Prophet ﷺ taught every one of them to us.

Lesson Two · What We Do

The Five Pillars of Islam

أَرْكَانُ الإِسْلام

In Lesson 1 we learned what a Muslim believes in the heart. Now we learn what a Muslim does with the body. Islam stands on five great actions, just like a roof stands on five strong pillars. If one pillar is missing, the building is not complete. These five are called the Five Pillars of Islam.

Shahada
Believing and saying that Allah is the only God and Muhammad ﷺ is His Messengerالشَّهادة
Salah
Praying to Allah five times every dayالصَّلاة
Zakat
Giving a small share of our wealth to those in needالزَّكاة
Sawm
Fasting in Ramadan, once we are old enoughالصَّوم
Hajj
Visiting the Kaaba in Makkah once, if we are ableالحَجّ
🏛️ Five pillars holding up one beautiful home — Islam
1 · Shahada — the words of a Muslim

The first pillar is the most important. When you say these words and believe them in your heart, you are a Muslim:

لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ مُحَمَّدٌ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ
La ilaha illa Allah, Muhammadun Rasulullah
"There is no god but Allah, and Muhammad ﷺ is the Messenger of Allah."
2 to 5 · Prayer, charity, fasting, pilgrimage

Salah is our prayer five times a day — a beautiful appointment to talk with Allah. Zakat means giving a little of what we own to the poor, so no one is left hungry. Sawm is fasting in the month of Ramadan; grown-ups stop eating and drinking from dawn to sunset, which teaches patience and thankfulness. Hajj is the great journey to the Kaaba in Makkah, made once in a lifetime by those who are able.

🕋
Did you know?

Wherever a Muslim stands to pray — in Kerala, in Makkah, or even on a mountain — they all turn to face the very same building: the Kaaba. At every moment of the day, somewhere on earth, someone is praying towards it.

Five Fingers, Five Pillars ✋

Your own hand can help you remember! Trace your hand on paper and label each finger with one pillar.

  • Thumb — Shahada (the strongest one, it comes first)
  • Index — Salah  •  Middle — Zakat
  • Ring — Sawm  •  Little finger — Hajj

Now the Five Pillars are always with you — right there on your hand!

🔑 New words to remember

Shahada شهادة — the words of faithSalah صلاة — daily prayerZakat زكاة — giving charitySawm صوم — fastingHajj حجّ — the pilgrimage
⭐
Remember

The Five Pillars are the actions that hold up a Muslim's life: Shahada, Salah, Zakat, Sawm and Hajj.

Lesson Three · Our Loving Allah

Getting to Know Allah

أَسْماءُ اللهِ الحُسْنَى

We believe in Allah — but what is Allah like? Allah is greater than anything we can picture in our minds. To help us know and love Him, Allah told us His beautiful names. There are ninety-nine of them, and each name is like a little window that lets us see something wonderful about our Creator. Here are eight to begin with.

الرَّحْمَٰن
Ar-RahmanThe Most Kind — His kindness is greater than any mother's or father's love.
الْخَالِق
Al-KhaliqThe Creator — He made the stars, the seas, every animal, and you.
الْعَلِيم
Al-'AleemThe All-Knowing — He knows everything, even the secret thoughts in your heart.
السَّمِيع
As-Samee'The All-Hearing — He hears every whisper and every quiet du'a you make.
الْبَصِير
Al-BaseerThe All-Seeing — nothing is ever hidden from Him, in light or in darkness.
الرَّزَّاق
Ar-RazzaqThe Provider — He gives food, rain, and every good thing we need to live.
الْغَفُور
Al-GhafoorThe Forgiving — whenever we truly say sorry, He forgives us with love.
الْوَاحِد
Al-WahidThe One — there is only one Allah, and no one and nothing is like Him.

Allah is not like anything we can imagine. He was not born, and He has no children. He never gets tired and He never sleeps. In one short surah of the Qur'an, Allah teaches us exactly who He is:

قُلْ هُوَ اللَّهُ أَحَدٌ ۝ اللَّهُ الصَّمَدُ ۝ لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ ۝ وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌ
Surah Al-Ikhlas — "Say, He is Allah, the One. Allah, the Eternal. He did not give birth, nor was He born. And there is none equal to Him."
🌍
Did you know?

You can spot Allah's names everywhere around you. A sweet mango to eat? That is Ar-Razzaq, the Provider. Rain that helps the paddy fields grow? That is His kindness, Ar-Rahman. The world is full of little signs of Allah.

Allah's Name Detective Hunt 🔍

For one whole day, become a "name detective"!

  1. Pick one beautiful name of Allah from above.
  2. Look around your home and outside. Find three things that remind you of that name.
  3. Draw them, and under each write: "This shows me Allah is ______."

For Al-Khaliq (The Creator) you might draw a tree, a butterfly, and the moon.

🔑 New words to remember

Asma-ul-Husna أسماء الحسنى — Allah's beautiful namesAr-Rahman — The Most KindAl-Khaliq — The CreatorTawhid توحيد — Allah is One
⭐
Remember

Allah is One. He has beautiful names that teach us about Him, and there is nothing at all like Him.

Lesson Four · Getting Ready

Wudu — Getting Clean for Prayer

الوُضُوء

Before we stand to pray our Salah, we make ourselves fresh and clean in a special way. This washing is called Wudu. It is not just ordinary washing — it prepares our body and heart to stand before Allah. The Prophet ﷺ told us that Wudu even washes away our small mistakes, like water washing dust from leaves. 🌿

Wudu has a beautiful order. Let's learn it step by step. Before you begin, make the intention in your heart that you are making Wudu for Allah, and say Bismillah.

1
🤲

Intention & Bismillah

Quietly decide in your heart: "I am making Wudu to pray for Allah." Then say Bismillah — "In the name of Allah."

2
🖐️

Wash your hands ×3

Wash both hands up to the wrists, three times, letting the water reach between your fingers.

3
👄

Rinse your mouth ×3

Take a little water into your mouth and swish it around, three times.

4
👃

Rinse your nose ×3

Gently sniff a little water into your nose and blow it out, three times.

5
😊

Wash your face ×3

Wash your whole face — from the top of your forehead to your chin, and ear to ear — three times.

6
💪

Wash your arms ×3

Wash your right arm up to the elbow three times, then your left arm three times.

7
🧑

Wipe your head ×1

With wet hands, gently wipe over your head once. This wiping is called masah.

8
👂

Wipe your ears ×1

With your wet fingers, wipe inside and behind both ears, once.

9
🦶

Wash your feet ×3

Wash your right foot up to the ankle three times, then your left foot three times. Don't forget between the toes!

🌟 A short du'a after Wudu
أَشْهَدُ أَنْ لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ وَأَشْهَدُ أَنَّ مُحَمَّدًا عَبْدُهُ وَرَسُولُهُ
"I bear witness that there is no god but Allah, and that Muhammad ﷺ is His servant and Messenger."
💧
Did you know?

Some things end our Wudu and we must make it again — like using the toilet, passing wind, or falling into a deep sleep. After that, we simply make Wudu once more before we pray. And remember: never waste water, even at the tap — the Prophet ﷺ taught us to use only a little.

Wudu Practice Chart 💦

Let's turn the nine steps into a fun chart!

  1. Draw nine little boxes and number them 1 to 9.
  2. In each box, draw the body part you wash in that step.
  3. Practise real Wudu with a grown-up, ticking each box as you go.

Doing the steps in the right order is part of a beautiful Wudu. Can you say all nine from memory?

🔑 New words to remember

Wudu وضوء — washing before prayerNiyyah نيّة — intentionMasah مسح — gently wipingBismillah بسم الله — "In Allah's name"
⭐
Remember

Wudu makes us clean and ready to pray. It has nine steps, done in order, beginning with intention and Bismillah.

Lesson Five · The Happy Month

Sawm — The Beautiful Month of Fasting

الصَّوم · رَمَضان

Once every year a very special guest arrives — the month of Ramadan. Muslim homes fill with excitement: lanterns go up, the smell of good food drifts from the kitchen, and families wake together before the sun. In this month grown-up Muslims keep the fourth Pillar of Islam: Sawm, which means fasting.

To fast means that from the first light of dawn until the sun sets, we do not eat or drink anything at all — not even water. We do it to obey Allah, to grow closer to Him, and to feel in our own tummies what a hungry person feels every day. It makes our hearts kinder and stronger. 💛

A day of fasting, step by step
1
🌙

Suhoor — the early meal

Before dawn, the whole family wakes up and eats a light meal called Suhoor. It gives us strength for the day. This is also when we make our intention (niyyah) in the heart to fast for Allah.

2
🌅

Fajr — the fast begins

When the Fajr adhan calls at dawn, we stop eating and drinking and pray the morning prayer. Now our fast has begun!

3
☀️

Daytime — a fast of the whole self

All day we take no food or drink. But fasting is more than that: we keep our tongue and hands good too — no lying, no fighting, no unkind words. We read Qur'an, help others, and stay cheerful.

4
🌇

Iftar — breaking the fast

At sunset (Maghrib) it is time for Iftar! Following the Prophet ﷺ, we break our fast with a date and some water, say our du'a, and then pray Maghrib. The first sip of water after a long day is pure joy.

5
🕌

Night — Taraweeh & togetherness

At night many Muslims pray special prayers called Taraweeh at the masjid, and families spend happy time together before resting for the next day's fast.

👨‍👩‍👧 Who has to fast?

Every healthy grown-up Muslim fasts the whole month of Ramadan. Children like you are not required yet — but many practise by fasting part of the day, and a little more each year, until you can fast the whole day. And Allah is always kind: those who are ill, travelling far, or very old do not fast then; they may fast later or feed someone in need instead.

What breaks the fast — and what doesn't
❌

These break the fast

Eating or drinking on purpose, or making yourself vomit, breaks the fast. If that happens, the day's fast is spoiled and must be made up later.

✅

These do NOT break it

Rinsing your mouth, brushing your teeth, having a bath or a swim, and smelling nice food are all fine. And if you forget and eat by mistake — your fast still counts! Allah fed you.

🚪
Did you know?

There is a special gate in Jannah (Paradise) called Ar-Rayyan. On the Last Day, only the people who used to fast will be invited to enter through it — and after they all pass, it will close. Fasting has its very own doorway to Paradise!

💛
Why we love Ramadan

The Prophet ﷺ said a fasting person has two joys: the happiness of Iftar, and a greater happiness when they meet Allah. Ramadan brings the family together at dawn and sunset, teaches us to share with the poor, and ends with the big celebration of Eid al-Fitr — new clothes, sweets, and thanks to Allah!

My Ramadan Star Chart 🌟

Make your own Ramadan chart to fill with good deeds!

  1. Draw 30 little stars in rows — one for each day of Ramadan.
  2. Each day, do one good deed (share food, help at home, read Qur'an) and colour a star.
  3. Help set the Iftar table — arrange the dates and water for your family.

Even if you fast only half a day, you are practising and growing. Every year you will become stronger!

🔑 New words to remember

Sawm صوم — fastingRamadan رمضان — the month of fastingSuhoor سحور — the meal before dawnIftar إفطار — breaking the fastEid al-Fitr عيد الفطر — the festival after Ramadan
⭐
Remember

Sawm is the fourth Pillar of Islam. In Ramadan we fast from dawn to sunset — growing our love for Allah and our care for others.

Lesson Six · Talking to Allah

Salah — The Daily Prayers & Friday Prayer

الصَّلاة · الجُمُعة

Imagine having a special appointment to talk with the King of everything — five times, every single day. That is Salah, our prayer, the second Pillar of Islam. In Salah we stand before Allah, thank Him, ask Him, and feel close to Him. It is a gift that keeps our hearts shining all day long.

Allah has given us five prayers, spread across the day like five little lamps — one at dawn, and the others through the day and night. Let's meet them.

The five daily prayers
🌄

Fajr الفجر

At dawn, before sunrise · 2 rak'ah

☀️

Dhuhr الظهر

Just after midday · 4 rak'ah

🌤️

Asr العصر

In the late afternoon · 4 rak'ah

🌇

Maghrib المغرب

Just after sunset · 3 rak'ah

🌙

Isha العشاء

At night · 4 rak'ah

How we pray — the four positions

Each part of the prayer is called a rak'ah, and in every rak'ah we move gently through four positions, each with its own beautiful name:

🧍 Qiyam قيام — standing 🙇 Ruku ركوع — bowing 🤲 Sujood سجود — prostrating 🧎 Julus جلوس — sitting

In Sujood, when our forehead touches the ground, we are at our very closest to Allah. 💚

🧠 Memorise the opening du'a — "Wajjahtu"

We say this quietly at the very start of Salah…

0 / 6 parts memorised
Jumu'ah — the special Friday prayer

Friday is the best day of the week for Muslims. On Friday, the midday Dhuhr prayer becomes a special prayer called Jumu'ah, prayed together at the masjid. First the imam gives a khutbah (a short talk to teach and remind us), and then everyone prays two rak'ah side by side, like one big family.

To get ready for Jumu'ah, it is lovely to follow the Prophet ﷺ: take a bath (ghusl), wear clean and nice clothes, put on a little perfume, go to the masjid early, and sit quietly to listen well to the khutbah.

💧
Did you know?

The Prophet ﷺ asked: if a river flowed at your door and you bathed in it five times a day, would any dirt stay on you? "None," said his companions. He said the five daily prayers are just like that — Allah washes away our little mistakes with each prayer. ✨

My Prayer Timetable ⏰

Make a prayer timetable and become the prayer-helper of your home!

  1. Write the five prayers in order: Fajr, Dhuhr, Asr, Maghrib, Isha.
  2. Ask a grown-up the time of each prayer in your town and write it beside each name.
  3. Tick each prayer you pray today — and try to remind your family too!

Can you also name the four positions and say Wajjahtu from memory?

Remember

Lesson Seven · The Two Holy Cities

Makkah & Madinah

مكة · المدينة

Of all the cities in the world, two are the most beloved to Muslims: Makkah and Madinah. Each city holds one of the greatest and most beautiful masjids on earth, and Muslims everywhere dream of visiting them one day. (Makkah is also the city where our Prophet ﷺ was born, and Madinah where he later lived.) Today, let's explore the two cities and their wonderful masjids.

Makkah & the Great Mosque

In the heart of Makkah stands the biggest masjid in the whole world — Masjid al-Haram, "the Sacred Mosque". It is so enormous that more than a million people can pray in it together! In the very centre of this masjid, in an open courtyard under the open sky, stands the most special building of all: the Kaaba.

The Ka'aba — the House of Allah

The Kaaba is a cube-shaped building, and it is the very first house ever built on earth for the worship of Allah. That is why we call it Baytullah — "the House of Allah". Long ago, Prophet Ibrahim and his son Isma'il (peace be upon them) raised its walls together.

The Kaaba is covered by a beautiful black cloth called the Kiswah, embroidered with golden words from the Qur'an. It has a tall golden door, and set into one corner is a special stone called the Hajar al-Aswad (the Black Stone). Wherever they are in the world, Muslims turn to face the Kaaba when they pray — this direction is called the Qibla. During Hajj and Umrah, pilgrims walk around the Kaaba seven times with love; this beautiful act is called Tawaf.

✨
Did you know?

The black cloth that covers the Kaaba — the Kiswah — is replaced with a brand-new one every single year! The old cloth is carefully cut into pieces. And the great door of the Kaaba is made of pure gold.

Madinah & the Prophet's Mosque

A few days' journey from Makkah lies the calm and green city of Madinah. Here stands the second most beloved masjid in the world: Masjid an-Nabawi, "the Prophet's Mosque". You can spot it at once by its famous green dome and its tall minarets. It was first built long ago and has been made larger and more beautiful over many, many years, until today millions can pray inside its calm and shining halls.

Draw the Ka'aba 🕋

Become an artist and draw the House of Allah!

  1. Draw the Kaaba as a tall black cube with a golden door.
  2. Add the golden band of writing around it (the Kiswah), and mark the corner of the Black Stone.
  3. Draw little arrows in a circle around it to show pilgrims doing Tawaf.

Can you point out which way the Qibla is from your own home?

Kaaba كعبة — the House of AllahBaytullah بيت الله — "House of Allah"Qibla قبلة — the prayer directionTawaf طواف — walking around the KaabaKiswah كسوة — the Kaaba's black clothMasjid al-Haram المسجد الحرام — the Sacred Mosque
⭐
Remember

Makkah is home to Masjid al-Haram and the Kaaba — the House of Allah that every Muslim faces in prayer. Madinah is home to Masjid an-Nabawi with its green dome. These are the two most beloved masjids in the world.

Lesson Eight · Allah's Own Words

The Holy Qur'an

القُرآن الكَريم

Imagine a letter written especially for you by the One who made the whole universe. That is the Qur'an — the true words of Allah, sent down to our Prophet Muhammad ﷺ through the angel Jibreel. It is the greatest and most precious book in all the world, and Allah has promised to keep it perfect and unchanged forever.

The Qur'an has 114 chapters, called surahs, and it is written in beautiful Arabic. Millions of Muslims — young and old — have even learnt the whole Qur'an by heart!

The Qur'an is the light of our life

The Qur'an is like a light that shows us the way in everything we do. It teaches us how to pray, how to be honest and kind, how to love and obey our parents, how to help those in need, and how to grow close to Allah. Whenever we feel sad or worried, the Qur'an brings comfort to our hearts — it is our very best friend for the whole of life.

That is why we try to read a little of the Qur'an every day, listen to it with love, understand its meaning, and do our best to live by it. A home in which the Qur'an is read is a home filled with light and peace. ✨

The wonderful rewards of the Qur'an

Allah is so generous that He rewards us for every single moment we spend with His book. Our Prophet ﷺ told us something amazing: for reading just one letter of the Qur'an, you receive ten good deeds (hasanat)! He said that "Alif-Lam-Mim" is not one letter — Alif is a letter, Lam is a letter, and Mim is a letter. So even those three little letters bring you thirty rewards.

🌟 More beautiful rewards

The Prophet ﷺ also taught us:
• "The best of you are those who learn the Qur'an and teach it."
• On the Last Day, the Qur'an will come and speak up for the friends who used to read it.
• Whoever reads the Qur'an beautifully is with the noble angels — and whoever finds it hard and keeps trying gets double the reward for trying!

💚
Did you know?

Because every letter gives ten rewards, reading even a short surah fills your book of good deeds with hundreds of rewards! Just saying Bismillah (19 letters) brings around 190 good deeds, in shaa Allah.

My Daily Qur'an Stars 📖

Start a beautiful daily habit with the Qur'an!

  1. Make a small chart with the seven days of the week.
  2. Each day, read a little of the Qur'an (even one line) and colour a star.
  3. Count the letters in one short line and multiply by ten — see how many rewards you earned!

A small amount every day is more beloved to Allah than a lot only once.

🔑 New words to remember

Qur'an قرآن — Allah's holy bookSurah سورة — a chapter of the Qur'anAyah آية — a verse (a line)Wahy وحي — revelation from AllahHasanat حسنات — good-deed rewardsHafiz حافظ — one who memorises the Qur'an
⭐
Remember

The Qur'an is Allah's own words and the light of our life. We read a little every day — and for every single letter, Allah gives us ten rewards.

Lesson Nine · Allah's Messengers

The Prophets of Allah

الأنبياء

Allah is so merciful that He never left people alone without guidance. To every nation, in every age, He sent kind and truthful teachers called prophets — in Arabic, a prophet is a Nabi. A prophet who was given a special message or holy book to share is also called a Rasul (a messenger). And every single prophet taught the very same beautiful message: worship Allah alone, and be good to people.

Allah sent a great many prophets — far more than we could ever count. But twenty-five of them are named for us in the Qur'an. Here they all are:

1آدمAdam(as) 2إدريسIdris(as) 3نوحNuh(as) 4هودHud(as) 5صالحSalih(as) 6إبراهيمIbrahim(as) 7لوطLut(as) 8إسماعيلIsma'il(as) 9إسحاقIshaq(as) 10يعقوبYa'qub(as) 11يوسفYusuf(as) 12أيوبAyyub(as) 13شعيبShu'ayb(as) 14موسىMusa(as) 15هارونHarun(as) 16ذو الكفلDhul-Kifl(as) 17داودDawud(as) 18سليمانSulayman(as) 19إلياسIlyas(as) 20اليسعAl-Yasa(as) 21يونسYunus(as) 22زكرياZakariyya(as) 23يحيىYahya(as) 24عيسىIsa(as) 25محمدMuhammadﷺ

The first prophet was Adam (as), and the very last is our own beloved Prophet Muhammad ﷺ — no prophet will come after him. Five of the prophets are called the Ulul-Azm, the greatest and most patient messengers: Nuh, Ibrahim, Musa, Isa, and Muhammad ﷺ.

🚢
Did you know?

Every prophet has a wonderful true story. Prophet Nuh (as) built a giant ark to save the believers from a great flood. Prophet Yunus (as) was kept safe inside a huge fish. Prophet Sulayman (as) could even speak with the birds and the ants! Each story teaches us to trust Allah completely.

Prophet Story Time 📖

Become a storyteller of the prophets!

  1. Choose one prophet from the list above.
  2. With a parent or teacher, learn one story about that prophet.
  3. Draw your favourite part, then tell the story to your family.

When we say a prophet's name, we say "alayhis salam" (as) — "peace be upon him". For our Prophet Muhammad we say ﷺ.

Lesson Ten · Made of Light

The Angels — Malaikah

الملائكة

Do you remember, from Lesson 1, that believing in the angels is one of the Articles of Faith? Angels — malaikah in Arabic — are wonderful creatures that Allah made out of pure light (noor). We cannot see them with our eyes, but they are always near. They never eat, never sleep, and never grow tired — and they always obey Allah, never once disobeying Him.

Allah created countless angels, far more than anyone could ever count. But some are named for us, and each one has a special duty. Here are ten of them:

1
📜

Jibreel جبريل

Brings Allah's words (revelation) to the prophets. He is the greatest angel.

2
🌧️

Mika'il ميكائيل

In charge of the rain and of provision (rizq) for all creatures.

3
🎺

Israfil إسرافيل

Will blow the great Trumpet (Sur) on the Last Day.

4
🕊️

Malak al-Mawt ملك الموت

The Angel of Death (Izra'il), who gently takes the souls.

5
❓

Munkar منكر

Questions people in the grave about their faith.

6
❓

Nakir نكير

The companion of Munkar, who also questions in the grave.

7
✅

Raqib رقيب

Writes down all the good deeds that we do.

8
📝

Atid عتيد

Writes down the mistakes we do, so we can turn back to Allah.

9
🔥

Malik مالك

The keeper and guardian of the Fire (Jahannam).

10
🌷

Ridwan رضوان

The keeper and guardian of Paradise (Jannah).

Two of these angels are especially close to you: Raqib and Atid, together called the Kiraman Katibin — "the honourable writers". They write down everything you do, the good and the not-so-good. So let's fill their books with beautiful deeds! 💚

✨
Did you know?

Angels love good gatherings! When people come together to learn about Allah or to read the Qur'an, angels gather around them, and Allah lovingly mentions those people to the angels. So a class like yours may have angels visiting it right now!

Angel Duty Chart ✨

Make a chart of the angels and their special jobs!

  1. Write the names of the ten angels down one side of a page.
  2. Beside each name, draw a little symbol for its duty (a raincloud, a trumpet, a pen...).
  3. Circle Raqib and Atid, and promise them a page full of good deeds!

Remember: angels are made of light and we cannot draw what they look like — so we draw their duties instead.
